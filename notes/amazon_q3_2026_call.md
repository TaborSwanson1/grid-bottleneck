# Amazon Q3 2026 earnings call (October 29, 2026)

Read from the Motley Fool transcript. Short phrases are quoted; everything else is paraphrased. Check exact wording against the transcript before quoting in a post.

## What they said

**Demand and capacity**
- Andy Jassy said AWS demand is still very strong and customers take new capacity almost as fast as Amazon installs it.
- Large customers reserve blocks of "hundreds of megawatts or sometimes up to a gigawatt" well in advance. Amazon keeps some capacity free for on-demand use.
- He said Amazon has good visibility into demand for 2027 and 2028, and part of 2029.
- Amazon is installing an AI accelerator about every 14 seconds.
- AWS grew 39.3% year over year, the sixth straight quarter of faster growth.

**Spending**
- Brian Olsavsky (CFO): Q3 cash capex was $75.9 billion, mostly AWS and AI infrastructure.
- Jassy: still expects about $220 billion of cash capex in 2026, and "meaningfully more" in 2027.
- Olsavsky named component pricing (including memory chips) as a margin headwind.

**Owned vs leased**
- Jassy said most of Amazon's capacity is "either owned or long leased," and argued that gives it a big cost edge over colocation and neocloud providers.

## What they didn't say

- **No mention of power or grid constraints** as a limit on growth on the call. "Energy prices" only appears in the standard risk disclaimer.
- The 10-Q does mention power once: Amazon signs energy contracts, some running 20 years, to "secure electricity supply" for existing and future operations. So Amazon locks power in years ahead, but it never calls power a constraint.
- **No mention of transformers or grid equipment.** The supply worries they did name were chips and memory.
- **No mention of hardware waiting for power** or the "Early Rack Receipt" practice from the Business Insider report.

## How this fits the project

- It cuts against the demand-slowdown story: demand is described as ahead of supply, with reservations years out, and capex is going up in 2027.
- It doesn't confirm the power-bottleneck story either. Amazon talks about being supply-limited, but it names chips, not power. Companies rarely volunteer that power is holding them back, so silence isn't proof either way. The lease-note data and the grid queue data (Day 8) have to carry that argument.
- The Business Insider claim about racks waiting for power stays "reported," not confirmed by Amazon.

## Reconciling capex

- The 10-Q reports **purchases of property and equipment of $77,259M** for Q3 (the number in `capex_quarterly.csv`).
- Amazon's "cash capex" on the call nets out **proceeds from property and equipment sales and incentives of $1,381M**: $77,259M - $1,381M = $75,878M, about $75.9B. It matches.
- So my series is gross purchases. That's fine for comparing companies, but say "purchases of property and equipment" when citing Amazon, or use the net figure to match what Amazon says.
- 2026 so far: $175.7B gross through Q3, against about $220B guided (which is net). That leaves roughly $50B for Q4, below Q3's pace.
